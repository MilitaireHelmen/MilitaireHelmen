The Netherlands Beret Technische Staf

The Netherlands Beret Technische Staf
 
het Dienstvak van de Technische Staf = the Service Section of the Technical Staff
 
Moto: Arte Pugnantibus Adsum:
Arte pugnatibus adsum" is a Latin expression and can be translated with:
The science is present with the combatants, or (as established by the Army order):
With professionalism I assist the warriors.
Artes = art, science
Pugnantes = the warriors
Adsum = be present, be there
 
 
Beret made by K.M.A. in the year 1988
 
 

 
Return to List of Berets
 
The Technical Service should not be confused with the Service Section of the Technical Staff (TS). The TS consists of higher technically (university) trained officers. This relatively small part of the Technical Troops often carries out projects such as tenders and modifications in the technical field. The TS also has a different beret emblem. This consists of the gearwheel containing a lion with a sword and arrows. The color of the logo background is red, in contrast to the black of the technical department.

Belgian Beret Insignias

Belgian Beret Insignias
 
 
 
This is what my Valère Wyckaert catalog says:
HAT CHARACTERS BELGIAN ARMY
period 1942 to 1990

The Belgian armed forces in Great Britain wore a police beret (English model) or a khaki beret and as a insignia  a Belgian lion (in bronze for officers and in copper for non-commissioned officers and troops)
The 1st Belgian Armored Car Squad had a black beret with a lion.
It was only from 1949 onwards that the different units were gradually issued with a different cap with their own cap badge. The infantry wore a light brown beret or cap (desert brown) with the insignia on a scarlet shield. Later a dark brown beret was worn (copied from the volunteers Battalion Korea)
The armored troops have a white metal insignia and a black cap. Certainly known are the burgundy colored (lie de vin) and green berets for the Paras and the Commands respectively.
September 1990 announces the gradual disappearance of the kaki beret when the Military Police (MP) a bright red beret; the logistic and administrative services receive a cobalt blue beret, the engineer (excluding the fighting engineers: black cap) and the transmission troops receive a pearl gray cap. The light aviation group gets a gray blue and the artillery retains its dark blue beret.
In the beginning the insignias were made in bronze, heavy in model. Now they are in brass (copper / zinc alloy) or white metal, making them light and pliable.
The colors of the metal are copper for the troop, silver for the non-commissioned officers and gold (yellow copper) for the officers
 
 
Dit is het kenteken van de 16de Geblindeerde Divisie gestationeerd in de B.S.D.
Werd gedragen met zwarte baret maar met verschillende schildjes onder het kenteken volgens de eenheid :

HK 16 DIV NEHEIM - wit schildje
17 Ps Bde SIEGEN - wit schildje
Cie HK en TR 16 DIV NEHEIM - huzaarblauw schildje
Comd Recce AROLSEN - lichtgeel schildje
Variatie 2 : kleinere kroon en kop

The Netherlands Beret Garde Regiment Jagers

The Netherlands Beret Garde Regiment Jagers
 
Jagers are Hunters and they were an infantry regiment part of the 12de Pantser Infanterie Bataljon, 7 December Divisie.
 
Beret made by ELO in 1981.
 


 
 
 
 
https://www.strijdbewijs.nl/diensttijd/jagers.htm
 
Return to List of Berets
 
Below comes from https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garderegiment
A Garde regiment is a regiment that has a separate status within the army.
The term guard indicates that this is an elite unit,
a unit that is better than the other units in terms of combat power, status, equipment, exercise, etc.
Garde regiments arose with the founding of standing armies, in the late 17th century.
Garde regiments were established by the head of state as a personal unit, charged with the protection of the head of state and the palaces.
Other regiments acquired that status later when they distinguished themselves in wars.
For the Garde regiments the best soldiers were often selected,
although many garment regiments only had a ceremonial function and the men were only selected for certain characteristics such as height.
Often the garde regiments were a breeding ground for officers for the rest of the army, as in Napoleon's guard.
Soldiers of wardrobe regiments were often distinguished by better equipment,
uniformity, armament and, of course, payment of ordinary soldiers.
In 1829 the Garderegiment Grenadiers and Jagers was established in the Netherlands,
which aimed to be an example for the rest of the army. A regiment that has earned its status as a guard is the Garderegiment Fuseliers Prinses Irene,
which continues the traditions of the Princess Irene Brigade, which was established in Great Britain during the Second World War and helped to liberate the Netherlands.

The Netherlands Beret Koninklijke Militaire School

The Netherlands Beret Koninklijke Militaire School or KMS
 
Koninklijke Militaire School = Royal Military School
https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koninklijke_Militaire_School_(Nederland)
 
In The Netherlands the KMS is the school for non-commissioned officers, In Belgium the KMS is the academy for the officers.
