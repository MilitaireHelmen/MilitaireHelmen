Dutch M53 helmet 1985 used by the Air Force (part 1)

Dutch M53 helmet 1985 used by the Air Force or "Luchtmacht" (part 1)
The Netherlands used the same helmet shells, same green color for all their forces, even for the Navy the helmet shells would be green. I know some of the navy helmets would be painted red, yellow or any other color but this was done by ship crews, however these helmets were issued in green.
 
The shell is made by VDN as shown by the heat stamp in the inside rim.
VDN 2/85
VDN = Vereinigte Deutsche Nickel-Werke.
2/85 = Februari 1985
VDN made helmets for several countries like this Belgian M1 helmet clone for the Air Force
 
The Liner in this helmet is Air Force grey. see liner used by the Air Force part 2

BEC = The plastic liners from N.V. BECCON.

v

 
 
NV BECCON
I found information about this company: www.berghapedia.nl/index.php/N.V._Beccon
 
N.v. Beccon, that existed from 1921 until 1993, was located in Doetinchem. One of the directors was Mr b. Becking. The name Beccon stands for Becking and Conijn. 
It was a metal pressing plant and emaillefabriek, and later also a galvanizing plant. There were petroleum and pans created, and further for example street signs. In a letter of 11 december 1936 to the municipality Bergh is informed that in note 18 street signs are taken according to the information you provide against the cleanliness of contents f. 1.25 per piece NET.
On 30 november 1944, towards the end of the second world war, the factory bombed by Allied aircraft. A Department that came with the Germans was in use, was destroyed, and part of the factory was in ashes. Director Becking is killed in this attack, as well as its Managing Director Schultz, a worker and three German soldiers. The day before was still present at the funeral in Becking Azewijn by Zeno Door Frost, which likewise factory Director was.
The company was, along with Vredestein, one of the first who are establised on the Hamburger industrieterrien pants. In 2004 has been in the hands of Intermeco. In 2008 are the premises, located a the north side of the track and to the East of the Misset Street, demolished.

USA M1 strange mix (part 3)

continued from USA M1 strange mix part 2
Here we can see the liner inside.
The suspension straps are made of cotton, woven in herringbone twill weave.
The color is almost tan so this is a late war time production.
 

picture 2 and 3 show the Firestone F and the number 40.
The 40 was the number of the mold used to make this helmet.

picture 4 and 5 show a print RAT KCOL, I haven't found what this stands for.

 The protective finish of the A washers before 1943 was chemical coatings
and than changed to green paint because of its lower cost.
after June 1944 the A washers were made in brass with black corosion inhibitor coating.
 
Found on: http://www.usmilitariaforum.com/forums/index.php?/topic/19550-marked-helmet-liners-the-unsung-hero-of-the-m-1/page-16
