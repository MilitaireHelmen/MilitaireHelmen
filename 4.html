Dutch M34 Helmet Restoration part 3

Now the helmet was ready to receive its new paint.
From a Dutch forum member a received the recipe / formula for the correct color.
This formula is for an Akzo-Nobel paint mixing machine:
WR 1 - 5,5
WW 1 - 4,75
WY 2 - 29,25
WZ 1 - 33,75

This is for 1/2 litre paint.
The base is COLORLESS, silk mat, oil based paint, I used Trimetal PU Satin.

French Adrian Helmet Model 15

This is the presentation of my first French Adrian Helmet model 15.
I forgot how I obtained it. It is in a very good condition but not complete, there is no liner and chin strap.

Germany Bodentruppenhelm 1A1 (M62)

Germany Bodentruppenhelm 1A1, sub model M62
This helmet has on top something that looks like a button.
At the inside the button is locked with a nut that holds the liner in place.
 
 
You can see two rivets on the side of the helmet, these are there to hold the chin strap.
I found the following insciptions in the liner:
8415-12-131-7859
SW- ? / 73
The first number is the NSN or Nato Stock Number.
The second line shows SW this stands for Schubert the 73 is the year of production 1973.

Germany Model 1A1 LL "Luftlandetruppenstahlhelm"

This German helmet is the model 1A1 used by the airborne troops or Luftlandetruppen.
This type of helmet has been in use since 1961.
Notice the blue/green camouflage I think this has been done by an induvidual soldier. I haven't found any information yet telling this was officially used by Germany or Belgian Airborne troops.
I'm thinking about Belgian troops based on picture 4. One can clearly see the place where I think was the Belgian flag. See my Belgian M71 with flag: Belgian Paratrooper helmet 1972
 

I found information showing this is a Belgian used 1A1 LL: An essential difference between the German and Belgian models is the small leather strap that turns the chin strap into a chin cup. The Belgians designated this helmet as the Model 71.
The helmet liner is dated September 1980 and made by, again, SW or Schubert Werke.
The Belgian paracommandos also used these helmets with a French style liner as shown in a previous articale:
http://helmliefhebber.skyrock.com/3288641706-Belgian-Paratrooper-helmet.html

British MKII BMB 1943

Here we see pictures of my British MKII 1943 made by BMB.
BMB stands for Briggs Motor Bodies of Dagenham Dock, Essex.
Briggs Motor Bodies From Graces Guide:
.
Branch factories in Southampton and Doncaster.
1930 Briggs Bodies Ltd was established to manufacture motor bodies, steel stampings, etc for Ford
1935 Public company was formed to acquired Briggs Bodies and the benefits of the patents, rights to designs, etc in Britain of Briggs Manufacturing Co of Detroit which manufactured for Ford and Chrysler. 4,500 employees. Manufactured also for Austin, Riley, Chrysler, Standard [1]
WWII Manufactured parts for the De Havilland Mosquito
1951 Together with Austin Motor Co. Ltd., Ford and Pressed Steel Co Ltd., Briggs provided additional capital for British Indestructo Glass by taking up an issue of debentures which gave them voting control.
1953 Acquired by Ford.

 
My helmet has been reused after the war by The Netherlands and has been refurbished for use by the civil defense or "Bescherming Bevolking".
If you want to read more about the BB se: https://anderetijden.nl/aflevering/588/De-BB-Bescherming-Bevolking
Many of British helmets that stayed in The Netherlands were used by The Netherlands army after WW2 and refurbished for use by the BB.
Up till now I haven't found a MKII used by The Netherlands army in its Original colors.
What can be found is many refurbished helmets who got painted green again by persons who want to make it look like an Original WW2 used helmet. This is a fact you need to know as a helmet collector.

Dutch made MKII

On of my first helmets in my collection is this Dutch made MKII.
The shape of the helmet shell and the liner is like the British MKII.
The chin strap is like the one used in the American M1 helmet and the Dutch model 53 helmet.
One difference between the Dutch and British MKII is the method used to attach the chin strap lugs to the helmets.
In the British MKII the chin strap lugs are attached with the help of rivets. The chin strap lugs are welded to the shell in the Dutch MKII.

These helmets are manufactured in the years 1955 and 1956 by the company Verblifa or VB or Verenigde Blikfabrieken.
I found Verblifa on https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VlzZsVAT9wM.

These helmets were made for the "Bescherming Bevolking" a Dutch organisation founded in 1952.
The organisation used many refurbished helmets used in WW2.

Additional information can be found at: https://dutchhelmets.nl part Bescherming Bevolking / civil defense

French Adrian Helmet Model 26 (1)

French Adrian Helmet Model 26.
This helmet was completed with a reproduction liner.
If you are interessted in a lot of detail see: world war helmets Casque Francais Adrian Mle 26
This is a big helmet, at the inside you can see a "D", this shel is suitable for liner sizes 60-62. 
The large ventilation hole under the comb, which had been a weak point of the old M15 design, was replaced with a series of small holes.
The comb of this helmet is made of aluminum. Based on this and shape of the comb the manufacturer of this helmet could have been: Dunois or Japy, I have a feeling it is made by Dunois.

French Helmet "Casque Troupes Toutes Armes Modèle 51 OTAN"

French Helmet "Casque Troupes Toutes Armes Modèle 51 OTAN" or "TTA 51"
After world war 2 the French came up with their own US M1 clone.
It looks a lot like the US M1 but the shape of the shell is different. The US M1 liner will not fit into the TTA 51.
This TTA 51 is a type 2, it has swivel bails to hold the chin strap. The type 1 has fixed bails like the first US M1 helmets.
My helmet is made in 1953 by S.A.U.F. Paris. I haven't found any information about this company.
 For a lot of additional information about this helmet see world war helmets Mle 51 TTA
